# Governance slices for issues #11–#15

Pure rules live in `Source/FamilyLife/Domain` and are covered by offline tests. The game layer (`Game/Governance*.cs`) adds the facts the domain cannot know (who is free, which maps exist, who is the Perspective Shift avatar) and the physical actions. Nothing below has been run in a game: compilation, the offline tests and the Harmony check are the only evidence. Setting: **Leadership, institutions and house politics** (off keeps only the records).

## What happens in the game

- **#11 planning card.** Each wedding, funeral, reunion and coming-of-age request gets a card (Families → Manage → Occasions). Time (bounded window), organizer, map/allowed area and guests have family-chosen defaults that confirm themselves at the deadline. The gathering uses those choices; a chosen area or meeting place becomes the preferred spot. The avatar is asked to lead, never assumed or directed.
- **#12 Steward duties.** One house per pass. The Steward (else the head) hears a petition, looks after a guest, mediates a rift, oversees a supply request, prepares union talks, negotiates a marriage alliance or organizes an occasion. Each counts only when its evidence exists: a finished ceremony, a recorded hearing, a one-time token from the finished meeting, or goods actually home. Interruptions are recorded as such. The optional meeting place must be a walkable cell inside the family's area; administrative talks, gatherings and polling prefer it and fall back safely.
- **#13 succession.** Runs for every house from the maintenance cycle (orderly, negotiation, compromise, dispute, branch). Candidate legitimacy and its reasons show in Families → Manage, filtered to what the viewer knows.
- **#14 crime families.** A house adopts theft only when need, a real target, permitting values, two willing members and a precedent all hold. The organizer recruits members one at a time by talking to them; each answers from their own bonds (join, refuse, expose). A ready job starts real thefts of real stock through the existing supply system; the job resolves from what that system recorded (goods home, witnesses, the theft incident). Heat rises when found out; a house can reform (restitution recorded by the supply system counts) or simply stop.
- **#15 institutions.** Government is no longer an overseer selector. A consensus colony that grows into several houses may develop a council movement; a mature council may seek elections; several houses may organize against an unpopular authoritarian ruler. A representative physically visits each house head, one active house gets one answer, and a majority must adopt the change within three days. A Perspective Shift avatar who heads a house answers for themself. Authoritarian rule still arises through a successful coup, not a peaceful menu choice. Voters walk to the polling place and cast their own ballots; absent people do not vote. A term end opens the next election. Aggrieved adults may scheme against the leader; recruitment and the final confrontation happen physically and resolve by capacity, not headcount.

## Also in the game

- **Protection deals.** A house whose past acts were mostly menace, with a poorly protected neighbour holding silver, may offer protection. The two heads talk; the neighbour accepts out of need (unsafe and trusts them) or fear (a coerced deal, which also puts an intimidation on record) or declines. Tribute is collected every few days through the supply system and recorded only when the silver really arrived. Reforming ends the deal.
- **Intimidation jobs.** The same job flow as theft, but the participants demand goods (the donor can refuse).
- **Councils.** A council names the colony's leader when the post is empty or the term ends; each seated house's head casts one vote by their own judgment.
- **Contraband, laundering and favors.** Willing-seller jobs and calling in favors. A house that holds stolen goods can sell them to a third house's member for silver, and the ownership record then follows the sale (so the original owner can no longer reclaim them through restitution). A house holding drugs can sell them to someone already dependent on them. Both use the normal barter flow and record no theft: nobody is wronged, but the house draws attention. A favor exists only because of a real recorded gift to someone in another house; calling it in is an ordinary request for goods, honoured or refused by the other side, and the outcome changes trust.
- **Council powers.** Under a council, a union of two houses needs the seated heads' approval (the first attempt raises the question, a later attempt goes through once it is approved; a union that would hold most of the colony's adults is refused), and a house of known bad conduct can be censured, which costs it standing. A head only votes on what they know; a head you control has a window to answer and is otherwise not counted.
- **Player-controlled pawns.** Questions put to a pawn you control (a scheme, a job, a protection offer, a request to hear, an open ballot) wait in the Families window under "Waiting for your answer". Nothing answers for them; an unanswered question counts for nothing.

## Community posts, camping and visiting kin

- **Community posts.** Four posts: the healer (the colony's doctor), the teacher, the hall keeper and the chaplain. The first three are chosen at a **yearly assembly**; the chaplain is whoever the ideoligion names as its moral guide (the one who converts and speaks for the faith), so that post simply follows the game and exists only with Ideology. A post carries public regard (prestige on being chosen, a little more for each real act, and a small lift to legitimacy), never a skill or work-priority change. Mediator, judge, quartermaster and envoy were set aside: none had evidence the game could honestly see, or a reason to exist that a person would recognise.
- **The yearly assembly.** Once a year (60 days; the first one a year after the colony has three adults) every elected post is put to the colony. The holder stands again if still fit and willing; others who are fit and willing may stand too. House heads publicly back whom they find best, leaning toward their own house; that nudges only the voters who trust that head. Every adult who is free walks to the polling place and votes in person for every post at once; the player's own pawn is asked, not sent. The count is announced with how each holder was judged. A post nobody is fit for is left as it is, and a vacancy between assemblies is filled by a quiet vote among those eligible. One person holds one post.
- **Why a person stands.** Nobody is drafted by a score. To stand, a person must be fit and willing and also have a reason: a **calling** (passion for the skill the post uses), a personal aim of **recognition** (being chosen and doing it well moves that aim along), a house that **sponsors** them (each house with a need the post meets puts forward its most capable fit member), or already **holding** the post. If nobody has a reason, the most capable fit person is asked as a **duty**, so a needed post is not left empty. The assembly letter says why each person stands.
- **One post per person.** A person stands for one post only: the one they hold, otherwise the one they are best at. The chaplain does not also run. A holder who is no longer fit or willing steps down at the assembly. The count never seats the same person twice.
- **Why a house wants one of its own.** Each house's ambition and situation say what it wants: medical-skill gaps, safety worries or hurt members want a doctor; children to bring on or a skill to teach want a teacher; hunger or a fractured house wants a hall; influence or legacy seekers want any post for the standing. The panel names these wishes. What the house gets is concrete: the doctor's kin are seen first and the check-up's recovery and immunity effect lasts twice as long for them; the teacher's kin are taught first and learn half as much again from each lesson; the hall keeper's kin are seated and served first. Kin served by one of their own feel it (a good thought), and the rest of the house is proud of kin who hold a post. Each act of the post also raises the house's standing, which feeds council seats, succession and election support. A house without a post gets none of this; there is no penalty for an empty post, only the service not happening.
- **What it does for the person.** Doing the work earns real trust and affection from those served (which later wins votes and counts in succession). The holder feels how it is going: trusted when they do it well, let down when they do not. Being chosen and doing it well advance a recognition aim.
- **How an incumbent is judged.** Their year is graded 0 to 1: half how much of the expected work they did (20 check-ups, 30 lessons, 30 meals, 4 rites), half how well they did it. Quality is per act: a lesson's skill gap, a check-up's skill and whether it was done in the medical room, a meal's grade, a rite's outcome. That grade tilts the vote: a good year gives a real lead (up to about +0.4 of a candidate's support score), a middling year is an even contest, a poor year is a handicap. A markedly better challenger can still win.
- **The hall keeper.** Every few days (3 by default, 1-10) the keeper tends the hall, the room with the most seats beside a table: sweeps it if it is dirty, then people who are hungry and free come and sit and wait, and the keeper fetches a meal from the stores and sets it on the table before each, in turn. Nothing starts unless the keeper is free; a diner who is not served within a while gives up and eats as usual. Credit is for dirt really cleared and meals really served.
- **The chaplain.** Leads rites: the colony's moral guide is preferred as a ceremony's organizer, and each rite organized is credited with its outcome. Not elected and never relieved for neglect.
- **The teacher's class.** By default every 3 days the teacher holds a class for the children: they teach each free child in turn, one real lesson at a time, passing on a skill they are clearly better at. The class waits (and is not used up) when nobody is free to learn, and a class that is not finished within a day is simply over. The player chooses how often (1-10 days), can switch classes off, and can allow adults to be students (off by default; an adult joins only when there is something to learn).
- **The doctor's check-ups.** Twice a year by default (1-4 a year, a year being 60 days) the doctor sees every colonist, one at a time, in the medical room (beside a medical bed, if the colony has one; otherwise wherever they are). Each person is seen once per round, only if free (the avatar is not called). The doctor tends anything that needs it, and the patient gets a recent check-up effect for about 20 days: faster natural healing and better immunity gain. A round lapses after 5 days if not everyone could be seen.
- **Camping trips.** A house with a real reason (a quarrel, a recent coming of age, a child to teach, a hearth custom, or warm ties) may wish for a one-to-two day trip within a few tiles of home. It is only a card: the player agrees or declines (declining costs nothing but a longer wait), forms the caravan with the game's own screen, and sends it. The trip counts only if two or more of the house are really at the place, stationary, for the whole stay. Leaving early ends it with nothing gained. Quality depends on how they get on, the weather, outdoors skill and any trouble between them. Steward never launches, moves or removes a caravan, and never suggests the avatar.
- **Visiting kin.** For a grand reunion the game confirms which relatives are safe to bring: alive, free to move, in a non-hostile faction, not a leader, not tied to a quest, not in a caravan or on another map. They are not touched until they walk onto the map. A trip may be held up on the road; a caravan that reaches the place within six days lets them continue, and if nobody comes the reunion goes ahead without them. Guests join the reunion, stay about three days, then leave by the map edge, which hands them back to the world. Relatives with no faction are not brought.
- **Council details.** A player-controlled head is asked for their leadership ballot (it waits two days; unanswered counts as absent). A union a head has accepted under a council is finished once the council approves it.

## Heirlooms and estates

**Status words used here.** *Implemented*: the code exists and both game versions build. *Offline verified*: domain tests, source-contract tests and a simulated save/reload pass. *Live verified*: played in RimWorld. Nothing in this section is live verified.

- **What can be personal (implemented, offline verified).** Only individually meaningful, non-fungible things: weapons, garments, books and works of art (a unique thing, never a stack, never a quest item or gear bonded to someone). Ordinary supplies (food, medicine, fuel, silver, any stack) stay communal property under the family stock claims and are **never divided between heirs**; this is a deliberate rule, not unfinished work. `StockClaim` is untouched: a thing the family's own stock claims for a different house is not recorded as someone's personal property.
- **Four different things are kept apart.** The family's stock claim, personal ownership of one item, the item's physical holder and place, and a testamentary expectation (a named heir) are separate records. A completed inheritance is only recorded when the item is physically in the heir's hands.
- **How something becomes a possession or heirloom.** A few colonists are looked at on each hourly pass (never the whole map): carrying a weapon, garment or book for two days makes it a possession (plain work clothes and scrap tools worth under 40 silver and below Good quality are nobody's own); fifteen days of sustained use makes a worthwhile one (market value 80 or quality Good or better) an heirloom. A very fine or valuable item (value 800, an art piece of 400, or Excellent quality worth 150) is a legacy item from the start. A wedding or coming of age marks what the person wore or wielded as an heirloom through a family memory. The owner, or the head of the owner's house, can declare an item an heirloom or house heirloom; heirlooms and ordinary possessions are listed under Governance, ordinary ones only to their owner. Identity is the game's thing id; an item that no longer exists is dropped on the next check.
- **Naming an heir.** An owner can name one person for one item or for everything they own, and say whether to tell them. A designation counts only if the heir knew (or someone else did) and the heir is still a living adult colonist; a private choice carries no weight with anyone else. Pawns name an heir for themselves now and then (the closest relative, usually told); the person you are playing is never chosen for: they use the Possessions list.
- **When someone dies (implemented, offline verified).** An estate is opened once, from the things the game can still find (on the body, in a grave, on a map, worn by someone). Anything it cannot find is dropped; nothing is made up. The head's office passes by the succession rules and does **not** pass the possessions: the new head is a claimant only on items declared house heirlooms.
- **Who can inherit.** A designated heir the owner told, then surviving spouse or partner, children (adopted children are children), parents, siblings and grandchildren, anyone who used or cared for the item, and for a house heirloom the head of its house. Claimants must be adults present in the colony; children and absent relatives do not claim. If nobody can take it, the house keeps it, and if there is no house either, it is recorded as having no heir.
- **Claims and disputes.** Each claim has a strength built from kinship, a known designation, closeness to the person who died, having used the item, the house's succession custom, and the house-heirloom role, less any tension with the owner. Ordinary items go to the strongest claim without fuss. A heirloom with two close claims, or one weaker claim held by a rival, becomes a **contested** case: mourning (2 days), claims raised (1 day), the family talks it over (2 days; a clearly weaker claimant without a rivalry may let it go), then mediation (the head if they have no stake, otherwise a Steward, otherwise whoever the claimants trust). A compromise spreads the contested items so nobody gets a second before everybody has one; a loser of a close single claim is either made Steward of their own house (if they are of it and the post is free) or keeps a lasting grievance (a recorded incident and a cooled bond). The case panel lists each claimant and their publicly known reasons; a designation is shown only to those who knew of it.
- **Physical delivery (implemented, offline verified as records only).** The heir (or, for a house, the head or a Steward) walks to the item, picks it up and carries it; it is kept in the heir's inventory or set down in the house's place. It is not a gift, a theft or a trade, and no supply request is involved. Nothing is moved from a living pawn: a thing someone else is wearing or carrying waits (10 days) and is then recorded as kept by them. A thing still on the body waits for the burial or for someone to strip it (8 days); the game never strips or buries anyone. A buried thing is recorded as buried with its owner. Forbidden things wait (10 days); a thing that cannot be reached is tried at growing intervals, five times at most. A destroyed or missing thing ends the case as lost; it is never replaced or teleported.
- **The person you are playing.** An heir who is the avatar is asked (take it or let it go; a weaker claimant is asked whether to press the claim), and a mediator who is the avatar chooses to approve the suggested spread, give an item to a claimant, or leave it to the family. Questions wait in "Waiting for your answer"; an unanswered question lapses after three days and counts for nothing: silence is not a yes, the item is not delivered and nobody else is handed it, and the case waits (up to 40 days) in case they answer late. The avatar collects an inherited item themselves from the same panel; the game never drives their pawn. Someone uninvolved sees only what they would reasonably know.
- **Limits, stated plainly.** Items are not scanned across the map; a record that was never seen on a colonist is not an heirloom. Gear that is buried with its owner is lost. Nothing is divided that cannot be a single physical thing. Caravans, other maps and mods that hide or remove things can make an item unfindable, and then it is recorded as lost. The wording of the heir questions is short and plain, not yet varied.

## The reunion rescue quest

- **Lifecycle (implemented, offline verified as records only).** A reunion's distant kin travel from the world. If the trip is held up, the game makes a real quest in the Quest tab naming the house and the relatives, with an offer that stands for three days. The player may accept or ignore it; nothing is accepted for them. Accepting makes a world site; a caravan has three days to reach it, and once it is there there is always at least a further day to put things right. The relatives are on the site's map as the same pawns (taken out of the world's keeping, never copied). When the trouble is dealt with they go back into the world's keeping and carry on to the reunion after a delay that fits the trouble; the reunion is rescheduled to wait for them. The kin visit record is the one authority: the quest and the site report what happened and the record decides, once.
- **Endings.** Rescue succeeded (the actual rescuers are recorded, their bonds with the rescued relatives improve, and a gift-like family memory is recorded); the offer lapsed or was ignored; the caravan left and the time ran out (abandoned); the quest was ended or declined; the site was destroyed; the whole party died. Each removes the party from the reunion's guest list, or only the dead from it on partial survival, and the reunion goes on. No ending can happen twice, and none leaves the reunion waiting. Quest, site and record are reconciled every hour, so a quest deleted by another mod or a site removed with the map is settled.
- **Trouble.** Chosen from real conditions where they exist: a breakdown (a broken wheel or a craft that cannot lift, by tech level), an injury, wild animals (where the land supports them), raiders (when a hostile faction exists) or bad weather (when it is cold or hot there). A relative is left in no danger the game cannot resolve: if the animals or raiders cannot be set up, the site is a plain breakdown. Success needs the trouble resolved (tended, hostiles gone, exposure recovered, or time spent helping) and nobody left downed.
- **Perspective Shift.** The quest arises on its own. A player-controlled organizer, head or relative sees the rescue line in the Governance panel but is never put in a caravan or made to accept.
- **Limits, stated plainly.** Map generation, the site's float menu, the quest tab text, the raiders' and animals' behaviour and the shuttle landing have not been seen in the game. Relatives still leave by walking off the map edge. The quest is created by the mod and is never offered by the storyteller.

## Not built

- Ordinary stacked supplies are not inherited by individuals: they stay communal by design.
- Gear is never taken from a living pawn, a body is never stripped, and nothing is replaced or teleported for an heir.
- Relatives leaving after a visit still walk off the edge; there is no departing-shuttle behaviour.
## Rules the tests pin down

- Ignoring a card never stalls an occasion; an invalid room, a lost organizer, a dead guest or a passed time repairs itself or ends the request cleanly.
- A Steward success needs evidence of a finished action, and the same evidence cannot count twice. The head covering for a missing Steward earns regard but adds nothing to a Steward's term. Ceremonies, aid, reconciliation by the rift system and negotiation are credited by the systems that perform them, so a duty does not credit them again.
- Legitimacy is separate from prestige, standing and office. A large family does not automatically produce a ruler. Members back different candidates for their own reasons. Private designations stay private.
- Crime needs all five conditions. Goods and tribute exist only with a one-time token or the supply system's own record. Witnesses know facts; reports and suspicion are never proof.
- Political support keeps ideology, family, legitimacy, friendship, fear, needs, policy and endorsement as separate measures. A smaller house can win through trust and conviction. A coup needs capacity (equipped, free people), not headcount.
