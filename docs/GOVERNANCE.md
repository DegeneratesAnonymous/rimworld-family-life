# Governance slices for issues #11–#15

Pure rules live in `Source/FamilyLife/Domain` and are covered by offline tests. The game layer (`Game/Governance*.cs`) adds the facts the domain cannot know (who is free, which maps exist, who is the Perspective Shift avatar) and the physical actions. Nothing below has been run in a game: compilation, the offline tests and the Harmony check are the only evidence. Setting: **Leadership, institutions and house politics** (off keeps only the records).

## What happens in the game

- **#11 planning card.** Each wedding, funeral, reunion and coming-of-age request gets a card (Families → Manage → Occasions). Time (bounded window), organizer, map/allowed area and guests have family-chosen defaults that confirm themselves at the deadline. The gathering uses those choices; a chosen area or meeting place becomes the preferred spot. The avatar is asked to lead, never assumed or directed.
- **#12 Steward duties.** One house per pass. The Steward (else the head) hears a petition, looks after a guest, mediates a rift, oversees a supply request, prepares union talks, negotiates a marriage alliance or organizes an occasion. Each counts only when its evidence exists: a finished ceremony, a recorded hearing, a one-time token from the finished meeting, or goods actually home. Interruptions are recorded as such. The optional meeting place must be a walkable cell inside the family's area; administrative talks, gatherings and polling prefer it and fall back safely.
- **#13 succession.** Runs for every house from the maintenance cycle (orderly, negotiation, compromise, dispute, branch). Candidate legitimacy and its reasons show in Families → Manage, filtered to what the viewer knows.
- **#14 crime families.** A house adopts theft only when need, a real target, permitting values, two willing members and a precedent all hold. The organizer recruits members one at a time by talking to them; each answers from their own bonds (join, refuse, expose). A ready job starts real thefts of real stock through the existing supply system; the job resolves from what that system recorded (goods home, witnesses, the theft incident). Heat rises when found out; a house can reform (restitution recorded by the supply system counts) or simply stop.
- **#15 institutions.** The overseer view can configure the starting institution (consensus, council, election or one ruler); this is deliberately unavailable while acting as a pawn. House heads can call elections and endorse by their own judgment. Voters walk to the polling place and cast their own ballot; absent people do not vote. A term end opens the next election. Aggrieved, unattached adults may start a scheme against the leader; the organizer recruits by talking, preparations are bounded and can be discovered, set back or reconciled, and the confrontation is a walk to the leader that decides the outcome by capacity, not headcount.

## Also in the game

- **Protection deals.** A house whose past acts were mostly menace, with a poorly protected neighbour holding silver, may offer protection. The two heads talk; the neighbour accepts out of need (unsafe and trusts them) or fear (a coerced deal, which also puts an intimidation on record) or declines. Tribute is collected every few days through the supply system and recorded only when the silver really arrived. Reforming ends the deal.
- **Intimidation jobs.** The same job flow as theft, but the participants demand goods (the donor can refuse).
- **Councils.** A council names the colony's leader when the post is empty or the term ends; each seated house's head casts one vote by their own judgment.
- **Contraband, laundering and favors.** Willing-seller jobs and calling in favors. A house that holds stolen goods can sell them to a third house's member for silver, and the ownership record then follows the sale (so the original owner can no longer reclaim them through restitution). A house holding drugs can sell them to someone already dependent on them. Both use the normal barter flow and record no theft: nobody is wronged, but the house draws attention. A favor exists only because of a real recorded gift to someone in another house; calling it in is an ordinary request for goods, honoured or refused by the other side, and the outcome changes trust.
- **Council powers.** Under a council, a union of two houses needs the seated heads' approval (the first attempt raises the question, a later attempt goes through once it is approved; a union that would hold most of the colony's adults is refused), and a house of known bad conduct can be censured, which costs it standing. A head only votes on what they know; a head you control has a window to answer and is otherwise not counted.
- **Player-controlled pawns.** Questions put to a pawn you control (a scheme, a job, a protection offer, a request to hear, an open ballot) wait in the Families window under "Waiting for your answer". Nothing answers for them; an unanswered question counts for nothing.

## Community posts, camping and visiting kin

- **Community posts.** Healer, teacher, quartermaster, mediator and judge are filled by the colony's own arrangement: everyone votes, the council's heads vote, or the leader appoints. Each voter backs whom they themselves find best (skill first, then trust, shared house, and no known misconduct). A post nobody is fit for stays empty; one person holds one post; a player-controlled pawn is never a candidate or a voter. A term lasts 30 days. A post carries public regard (prestige on election, a little more for each real act, and a small lift to legitimacy), never a skill or work-priority change.
- **Evidence for a post.** Healer: the pawn's own tended-patient record. Teacher: a lesson the game saw (the teacher's own job, or an existing teaching conversation with a child). Quartermaster: a supply request the supply system finished that the holder carried. Mediator: a quarrel actually mediated. Judge: an in-person hearing. A token is counted once, and at most three acts count in a day. Work that is needed and ignored for 15 days loses the post (and the holder cannot be chosen again at once); a quiet stretch with nothing to do never does.
- **The judge.** Only an accusation the judge knows first-hand can be upheld; one about their own house, one they are party to, one already ruled on and one already settled are not heard. An upheld finding costs the accused house a little standing and raises its heat. Suspicion never convicts.
- **Camping trips.** A house with a real reason (a quarrel, a recent coming of age, a child to teach, a hearth custom, or warm ties) may wish for a one-to-two day trip within a few tiles of home. It is only a card: the player agrees or declines (declining costs nothing but a longer wait), forms the caravan with the game's own screen, and sends it. The trip counts only if two or more of the house are really at the place, stationary, for the whole stay. Leaving early ends it with nothing gained. Quality depends on how they get on, the weather, outdoors skill and any trouble between them. Steward never launches, moves or removes a caravan, and never suggests the avatar.
- **Visiting kin.** For a grand reunion the game confirms which relatives are safe to bring: alive, free to move, in a non-hostile faction, not a leader, not tied to a quest, not in a caravan or on another map. They are not touched until they walk onto the map. A trip may be held up on the road; a caravan that reaches the place within six days lets them continue, and if nobody comes the reunion goes ahead without them. Guests join the reunion, stay about three days, then leave by the map edge, which hands them back to the world. Relatives with no faction are not brought.
- **Council details.** A player-controlled head is asked for their leadership ballot (it waits two days; unanswered counts as absent). A union a head has accepted under a council is finished once the council approves it.

## Not built

- Inheritance of anything beyond the office of head is deliberately not attempted.
- Changing the colony's constitutional form through an organic in-world campaign. The current selector is an overseer-only starting/setup control.
## Rules the tests pin down

- Ignoring a card never stalls an occasion; an invalid room, a lost organizer, a dead guest or a passed time repairs itself or ends the request cleanly.
- A Steward success needs evidence of a finished action, and the same evidence cannot count twice. The head covering for a missing Steward earns regard but adds nothing to a Steward's term. Ceremonies, aid, reconciliation by the rift system and negotiation are credited by the systems that perform them, so a duty does not credit them again.
- Legitimacy is separate from prestige, standing and office. A large family does not automatically produce a ruler. Members back different candidates for their own reasons. Private designations stay private.
- Crime needs all five conditions. Goods and tribute exist only with a one-time token or the supply system's own record. Witnesses know facts; reports and suspicion are never proof.
- Political support keeps ideology, family, legitimacy, friendship, fear, needs, policy and endorsement as separate measures. A smaller house can win through trust and conviction. A coup needs capacity (equipped, free people), not headcount.
